Community College of Philadelphia (CCP) is the city's public two-year college. It admits any student with a high-school diploma or equivalent, offers associate degrees, certificates and non-credit workforce programs, and is the largest public provider of higher education in the city by headcount of local residents. It is one of Pennsylvania's community colleges, with the City of Philadelphia acting as its local sponsor. [1]

1 Governance and sponsorship

Under Pennsylvania's Community College Act, a community college is created by a sponsoring local government. CCP was established in 1964 with the City of Philadelphia as its sponsor, and its first classes began in September 1965 in a former downtown department-store building under founding president Allen T. Bonnell. Today a Board of Trustees, appointed through the City as the college's sponsor (see the mayor's role in local appointments), governs the college, sets tuition, approves budgets and hires the president. The college is a separate legal entity from both the City and the School District of Philadelphia, and its employees are represented by unions including a faculty and staff federation. [1]

2 Funding

Pennsylvania community colleges are funded in roughly three parts: student tuition and fees, state operating appropriations, and sponsor (local) support. In CCP's case the City contributes an annual appropriation through the City budget, and the college has also relied on state capital programs and federal Pell Grant revenue. Amounts change annually and are published in the City budget and the college's own financial statements. Because many students are low-income, adult or part-time, enrollment and revenue respond to labor-market conditions and to financial-aid policy. [2]

3 Leadership change 2025

In April 2025 the Board of Trustees voted unanimously not to renew the contract of Donald "Guy" Generals, who had been president since 2014; the board cited performance discrepancies and a growing misalignment with board expectations, and local press noted the absence of detailed public explanation. Alycia Marshall, the provost, became interim president. In May 2025 WHYY reported that a judge had denied an effort to reinstate Generals. After a national search with four finalists, the Board announced her appointment as the college's seventh president on October 21, 2025, and the Inquirer reported in December 2025 that the board approved a contract for her. [3]

4 Role in workforce and city policy

CCP is a major partner in city workforce efforts, offering job-training programs and pipelines into health care, trades, public safety and information technology. It coordinates with the Office of Workforce Development, employers and unions, and it is a common landing point for city-supported scholarship and last-dollar aid initiatives. It also provides adult-education and English-language programs and works with the District on dual-enrollment for high-school students.

5 Who holds influence

The City, as sponsor, appoints the trustees, and City Council shapes the appropriation, so City Hall holds substantial leverage. The state legislature sets operating support, and employers and philanthropies influence program priorities through grants (see Philanthropy in Philadelphia). Faculty and staff unions have a voice in bargaining and public testimony, and student groups participate in trustee meetings, which are open under the Sunshine Act.
