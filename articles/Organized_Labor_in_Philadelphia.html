Organized Labor in Philadelphia
TypeCivil-society sector (private and public-sector unions)
Central bodyPhiladelphia Council AFL-CIO (over 100 local unions, per City & State, 2026)
Largest city-government unionsDC 33, DC 47, FOP Lodge 5, IAFF Local 22
Governing lawNational Labor Relations Act (private sector); Pennsylvania Act 111 and Act 195 (public sector)
Recent flashpoint2025 DC 33 strike (July 2025)

Organized labor in Philadelphia consists of the unions that represent workers in city government, the school district, transit, construction, health care, hospitality, building services and other sectors, together with the councils that coordinate them. Philadelphia is a heavily unionized city by American standards, and because it is both a city and a county, its municipal government is one of the region's largest union employers. The relationship between unions and elected officials shapes budgets, hiring, public safety policy and development.[1]

1 Historical background

Philadelphia has a long labor history. The 1806 Philadelphia cordwainers (shoemakers) conspiracy trial is a landmark in early American labor law, and the city saw a 1910 general strike and major transit and industrial disputes in the twentieth century. As manufacturing declined, union strength shifted toward the public sector, the building trades, health care and education. Municipal workers gained bargaining rights under state law in the 1960s and 1970s, and city employee strikes, such as the DC 33 strike of 1986, have periodically shaped city politics. The most recent was the July 2025 strike, covered in 2025 DC 33 Strike.

2 Public-sector unions

The city's civilian workforce is mainly represented by two AFSCME councils: District Council 33, which represents roughly 9,000 blue-collar and related workers, and District Council 47, which represents white-collar, professional and supervisory employees. Uniformed workers bargain separately: FOP Lodge 5 for police and IAFF Local 22 for firefighters and paramedics. Under Pennsylvania law, police and firefighters cannot strike and instead use binding interest arbitration, while most other public employees may strike under defined conditions; see Public Sector Bargaining in Pennsylvania. The Philadelphia Federation of Teachers represents roughly 14,000 school district employees.

3 Building trades and construction

Construction unions are organized through the Philadelphia Building and Construction Trades Council, which represents more than 50 affiliated unions according to 2026 reporting, and individual locals such as IBEW Local 98. Their influence over public works, development and zoning debates is described in Prevailing Wage and Project Labor Agreements.

4 Transit, education, health care and service workers

Transit workers at SEPTA are largely represented by TWU Local 234; see also SEPTA. Teamsters locals cover trucking, warehousing, school bus and other work. Hospital, nursing home, building-service and hotel workers are covered in Service and Health Care Unions. Large private employers, including the universities and hospital systems described in Eds and Meds Anchor Institutions, have seen growing organizing activity in recent years.

5 Coordinating bodies and advocacy

The Philadelphia Council AFL-CIO coordinates political and community campaigns among its affiliates. Outside traditional unions, worker centers and advocacy groups organize workers who lack a union contract; see Worker Centers and Labor Advocacy. City enforcement of local labor standards is handled by the Office of Worker Protections.

6 How unions interact with the city

City unions bargain with the mayor's administration, whose labor negotiators work with the Office of Human Resources and the Law Department, within a budget overseen by the Finance Department and reviewed by PICA. Council approves the funding. Unions also endorse and campaign in elections; see Labor and Politics in Philadelphia. Contract outcomes are widely reported and compared across unions, as in the 2025-2026 coverage of police, firefighter and civilian settlements.
