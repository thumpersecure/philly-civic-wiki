Free Library of Philadelphia

The Free Library of Philadelphia is the city's public library system. It operates the Parkway Central Library on the Benjamin Franklin Parkway, regional libraries and neighborhood branches, offering lending, internet access, programs, literacy and workforce services, and community space. Kelly Richards has been President and Director since 2022.[1]

1 Governance and legal basis

The Free Library was chartered in 1891 as “a general library which shall be free to all,” and is established under the Home Rule Charter, which gives it a board of trustees and a director. Although it is a city institution funded in the city budget, it functions with considerable independence from mayoral departments. The Free Library Foundation raises private funds to support programs and collections. Most city library staff are covered by municipal union contracts (see District Council 33 and District Council 47).

The Parkway Central Library opened on June 2, 1927, at 1901 Vine Street on Logan Square, in a Beaux-Arts building designed by Julian Abele and modeled on Parisian landmarks on the Place de la Concorde; construction had been planned since 1911 and was delayed by World War I.[4] The system holds about 4.24 million items and includes the Rosenbach Museum and Library among its locations, according to a summary on Wikipedia. Philadelphia City Council's June 2026 announcement of the FY27 budget listed $33 million for library HVAC improvements among its investments.[5]

2 Services

Services include book, media and digital lending, free computer and Wi-Fi access, children's and teen programming, English-language and adult literacy classes, job and small-business help, and rooms for community meetings. Library cards are free to anyone who lives, works, attends school or owns property in Pennsylvania. The library's Rare Book Department and special collections are citywide cultural assets, as are the Parkway Central Library's historic reading rooms. Libraries have also been used as cooling or warming locations during extreme weather, in coordination with the Office of Emergency Management.

3 Funding and hours

Most of the system's funding is the city appropriation, supplemented by state aid and private donations. The mayor's proposed budget for fiscal year 2027 allotted about $78 million to the Free Library, and Richards testified that up to nine libraries, including Parkway Central, the three regional libraries (Northeast, Lucien E. Blackwell West Philadelphia, and Joseph E. Coleman Northwest) and up to five neighborhood libraries, could add Sunday hours if the proposal were approved.[2] Under Richards, expanding hours has been a priority: all operating libraries are now open on Saturdays, hours that were cut during the pandemic.[2] Budget requests are decided by City Council in the spring.

4 Capital improvements and partners

Branch renovations have drawn on the Rebuild program funded by the city's beverage tax (see Philadelphia Taxes), as well as city capital funds and philanthropy. The library works with the School District of Philadelphia and community organizations on student access and literacy, and with Parks and Recreation on shared neighborhood facilities.

5 Access and accountability

Hours, building condition and staffing are the most frequent subjects of public comment. Residents can contact the library or branch managers, testify at Council budget hearings (see How to Testify at City Council), or submit service issues through Philly311. Advocates have repeatedly asked the mayor and Council for increased funding for libraries and parks.[3]
