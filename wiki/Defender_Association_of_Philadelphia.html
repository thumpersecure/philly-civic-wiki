Defender Association of Philadelphia

Defender Association of Philadelphia

The Defender Association of Philadelphia is the public defender for Philadelphia County. It is a private nonprofit corporation, not a city department, that contracts with the City of Philadelphia to provide constitutionally required counsel to people who are charged with crimes and cannot afford an attorney. Reference listings date its founding to 1934, which would make it among the oldest public defender organizations in the United States. [1]

1 Legal basis

The Sixth Amendment, as interpreted by the U.S. Supreme Court in Gideon v. Wainwright (1963), requires states to provide counsel to defendants in serious criminal cases who cannot pay. Pennsylvania has historically left the funding of indigent defense primarily to counties, and this arrangement has drawn attention from legal advocates and courts. In Philadelphia the Defender Association is the primary provider. Cases in which the Defender Association has a conflict of interest, such as when it represents a co-defendant or a witness, are assigned to private court-appointed attorneys through the courts.

2 Structure and leadership

The Defender Association is led by a Chief Defender and governed by a board of directors. The organization's website identifies Keisha Hudson as Chief Defender. She follows Keir Bradford-Grey, who became head of the association in 2015. [2] The organization represents adults and juveniles and also provides child advocates in dependency matters; its website describes an Immigration Unit and says the team has filed more than 396 petitions in the federal Eastern District of Pennsylvania since February, helping secure the release of more than 290 immigrants from detention. The organization also employs investigators, social workers and other support staff. The Defender Association is a major employer of lawyers in the city and a training ground for criminal defense attorneys.

3 Funding

Most of the organization's budget comes from a city contract appropriated through the annual budget process; see Philadelphia City Budget Process. Like other agencies that receive city money, it appears in the annual budget process. [2] Because the contract is negotiated with the city and defense lawyers oppose the prosecutor and sometimes the city's own police, the funding relationship is a recurring subject in discussions of independence. The association also receives some state and private grant funding for specific programs.

4 Role in the system and reform work

The Defender Association represents clients from arraignment through trial and appeal, and its lawyers frequently appear in bail hearings, preliminary hearings, and probation violation hearings. It has been a party to discussions with the District Attorney, the courts and the Department of Prisons about pretrial detention, diversion, and the reduction of the jail population. It also advocates on policy through its testimony and public reports, including on probation reform; see Adult Probation and Parole Department. Other legal-services organizations that assist residents in civil matters are described in Legal Aid and Public Interest Law.

5 How to reach a defender

People who are arrested are typically assigned a defender at arraignment. Eligibility is determined by income and the seriousness of the charge, and a judge may order a defendant to repay costs in some circumstances. Family members can contact the organization's office for general information, but the Defender Association cannot discuss a client's case without the client's consent. The organization does not handle most civil matters such as landlord-tenant disputes; for those, see Eviction Diversion and Landlord-Tenant Court and Tenant and Housing Advocacy Groups.

See also: First Judicial District, Philadelphia District Attorney's Office, Legal Aid and Public Interest Law, Philadelphia Department of Prisons.
