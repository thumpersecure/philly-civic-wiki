Landlord and Property Owner Associations

From the Philadelphia Civic Wiki, the free guide to how the city works
Landlord and Property Owner Associations
TypePrivate trade and advocacy associations
Key groupsHAPCO Philadelphia (reported about 1,000 members; largest advocacy group for independent landlords); Pennsylvania Apartment Association; Greater Philadelphia Association of Realtors; Building Industry Association
Primary arenasCity Council, Licenses and Inspections, Municipal Court, state legislature
Related lawPhiladelphia rental-licensing and Safe and Healthy Homes provisions; Pennsylvania Landlord and Tenant Act

Landlord and property owner associations represent people and firms that own rental housing in Philadelphia, from small owners with a single rental property to large multifamily operators. They provide members with education, forms and legal updates, and they lobby on laws and regulations affecting rental housing. They are private organizations. [1]

1 Main organizations

HAPCO Philadelphia, historically the Home and Property Owners Association, describes itself as a membership group for independent landlords; WHYY reported it as the city's largest advocacy group for such owners, with about 1,000 members. The Pennsylvania Apartment Association represents multifamily owners and managers statewide. The Greater Philadelphia Association of Realtors and the Building Industry Association also engage on housing policy (see Developers and the Real Estate Lobby). Larger management companies sometimes lobby directly. [2]

2 What they do

Associations offer members training on leases, screening, rental licensing and eviction procedure, discounts on services and legislative alerts. They testify at City Council hearings and meet with elected officials and agencies such as the Department of Licenses and Inspections. They typically support streamlined court procedures and oppose measures they view as raising costs or limiting owners' discretion, while many also state support for safe housing and for cooperation with code enforcement. They can also collect political contributions subject to the rules described in Campaign Finance in Philadelphia.

3 The Safe and Healthy Homes dispute

The Safe and Healthy Homes Act bills, sponsored by Councilmember Nicolas O'Rourke, would entitle tenants to rent abatement if a landlord lacks an active rental license or fails to repair code violations in a timely manner, add protections from retaliation, require good cause for non-renewal and increase penalties for repeated violations. HAPCO opposed the legislation. In March 2026 two landlords, both HAPCO members but not HAPCO itself as an organization, sued, alleging that Council violated the Sunshine Act by opening public comment only after the Housing Committee had passed the bills. The suit temporarily blocked the Council vote; after a settlement, the bills returned to committee for a new hearing and the final bills passed in April 2026. Reporting also described escalating confrontation between the landlord group and supporters of the legislation. [3]

4 Perspectives and counter-arguments

Owner groups argue that small landlords face thin margins, that new mandates may lead some to sell or leave the market and that enforcement should target negligent operators rather than all owners. Tenant advocates and legal-aid organizations (see Tenant and Housing Advocacy Groups) argue that habitability and anti-retaliation protections are necessary because many tenants cannot move. Council and the mayor weigh these positions; in this case Council passed the legislation. Landlord interests are not uniform: nonprofit and affordable-housing owners may take different positions from market-rate owners.

5 Practical information for owners

Owners must hold a rental license and meet code requirements; see Rental Licensing and Landlord-Tenant Law in Philadelphia, Eviction Diversion and Landlord-Tenant Court and the Fair Housing Commission. This is general information, not legal advice.

See also: Tax Abatement and Development Incentives, Office of Property Assessment, Sunshine Act and Open Meetings.
