Schools, Business and Civil Society

From the Philadelphia Civic Wiki, the free guide to how the city works

Schools, business and philanthropy, advocacy groups, media, neighborhoods and who holds influence.

Articles (19)

  • School District of Philadelphia — The city's public school system, the largest in Pennsylvania, governed by a mayor-appointed Board of Education and led by Superintendent Tony Watlington.
  • Philadelphia Board of Education — The nine-member, mayor-appointed body that governs the School District of Philadelphia, replacing the state-dominated School Reform Commission in 2018.
  • Charter Schools in Philadelphia — Publicly funded, independently operated schools that enroll about a third of Philadelphia's public-school students and are authorized by the School District.
  • Community College of Philadelphia — The city's public, open-admission community college, sponsored by the City of Philadelphia and led since October 2025 by President Alycia Marshall.
  • Chamber of Commerce for Greater Philadelphia — The region's largest business membership and advocacy organization, led by President and CEO Chellie Cameron, and a major voice on city tax and economic policy.
  • Center City District and Business Improvement Districts — How Philadelphia's property-assessment-funded business improvement districts, led by the Center City District, supply cleaning, safety and planning services.
  • Eds and Meds Anchor Institutions — Penn, Temple, Jefferson, Drexel, CHOP and other universities and hospitals that are Philadelphia's largest employers, landowners and tax-exempt institutions.
  • Philanthropy in Philadelphia — The foundations, donor networks and nonprofits, including William Penn Foundation and the Lenfest Institute, that fund civic work and shape policy priorities in Philadelphia.
  • Developers and the Real Estate Lobby — The builders, realtors, brokers and trade groups that lobby Philadelphia government on zoning, permits, tax abatement and housing policy, and how they interact with Council.
  • Community Development Corporations — Neighborhood-based nonprofits that build affordable housing, run commercial corridor programs and organize residents, and their citywide association PACDC.
  • Civic and Neighborhood Associations — Volunteer block, civic and neighborhood groups, many of them Registered Community Organizations, that review zoning proposals and lobby District Council members.
  • Faith Communities and Black Clergy — The role of congregations, interfaith coalitions and Black clergy organizations in Philadelphia politics, community services and public-safety debates.
  • Good Government Groups — Nonpartisan watchdog and civic-reform organizations in Philadelphia, led by the Committee of Seventy, that monitor elections, ethics and public spending.
  • Legal Aid and Public Interest Law — The nonprofit law offices that provide free civil legal help and impact litigation in Philadelphia, including Community Legal Services and the Public Interest Law Center.
  • Tenant and Housing Advocacy Groups — Tenant unions, legal-aid-linked networks and housing coalitions that organize renters in Philadelphia and pressed for the Safe and Healthy Homes Act.
  • Landlord and Property Owner Associations — HAPCO Philadelphia and other groups representing rental property owners, and their role in housing legislation including the 2026 Safe and Healthy Homes Act dispute.
  • Local News Media in Philadelphia — The Inquirer, Philadelphia Tribune, WHYY and other outlets that cover city government, who owns them, and how their finances and reach have changed.
  • Sports Teams and Public Subsidy — How the Phillies, Eagles, Sixers and Flyers relate to city and state government, from publicly assisted stadiums to the privately financed South Philadelphia arena planned for 2030.
  • Philadelphia Neighborhoods Guide — A resident's map of Philadelphia's neighborhoods, the 18 planning districts, the ten Council districts and how neighborhood boundaries relate to government.
