Labor and Politics in Philadelphia

From the Philadelphia Civic Wiki, the free guide to how the city works

Labor and politics in Philadelphia describes how unions influence elections, officeholders and policy in a city where organized workers are numerous and the municipal government is itself a large unionized employer. Unions act in three ways: as bargaining counterparts of the city, as political organizations that endorse and mobilize, and as lobbyists on specific legislation.[1]

1 Unions as bargaining counterparts

The mayor negotiates with unions whose members elected, or could vote against, the mayor. In 2025 the Parker administration settled with DC 47, the DC 33 strike, and the FOP in the same summer; see DC 47 and FOP Lodge 5. City Council sets the budget in which settlements are funded, and PICA reviews the five-year plan.

2 Endorsements and mobilization

Union endorsements in mayoral, Council, District Attorney and statewide races provide money, volunteers and credibility; see Elections in Philadelphia and Political Action Committees and Big Money. The Philadelphia Council AFL-CIO coordinates much of this. In September 2026 FOP Lodge 5 endorsed Gov. Josh Shapiro for a second term, and in August 2026 the Building Trades Council campaigned against Republican gubernatorial candidate Stacy Garrity. In July 2026, after an arbitration award that the union considered inadequate, IAFF Local 22 president Mike Bresnan said the union would approach prospective mayoral candidates, which the Inquirer described as a shift for a traditionally less political union.

3 The building trades and development

The Building Trades have significant influence over development: their support or opposition affects proposals, subsidies and zoning fights, and they press for union labor requirements discussed in Prevailing Wage and Project Labor Agreements. See Developers and the Real Estate Lobby.

4 Corruption cases and oversight

The most prominent labor-related corruption case involved IBEW Local 98. Former business manager John Dougherty was convicted in 2021 of a scheme that included bribery of Councilmember Bobby Henon, and in 2023 of embezzlement from the union. He was sentenced to six years in July 2024; former president Brian Burrows received four years. These cases are summarized in Political Corruption Cases in Philadelphia. City rules on contributions and conflicts are described in Campaign Finance in Philadelphia and Board of Ethics.

5 Recent developments

In 2025 and 2026 labor shaped the agenda on police discipline (The FOP Contract and Police Discipline), municipal wages, worker protection law (Office of Worker Protections) and transit (TWU Local 234). Critics argue that union influence can limit reform or raise costs; supporters argue it secures fair wages and public services. Both are positions attributed to participants in these debates.
