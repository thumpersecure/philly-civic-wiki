Administration and Finance

From the Philadelphia Civic Wiki, the free guide to how the city works

The departments that run the city itself: money, taxes, procurement, HR, law, technology.

Articles (21)

  • Finance Department — The Office of the Director of Finance, the City of Philadelphia's chief financial office, which runs the budget, accounting, payroll, risk management and oversees Revenue, the Treasurer, OPA and Pensions.
  • Revenue Department — The City agency that bills and collects Philadelphia's taxes, water bills and other charges, runs the Philadelphia Tax Center and administers tax and water assistance programs.
  • Office of Property Assessment — The City agency that determines the market value of every property in Philadelphia, the base for real estate taxes that fund schools and city services; includes how to appeal a value.
  • Board of Revision of Taxes — The independent board that hears appeals of Philadelphia property assessments and certain tax-exemption and abatement disputes after the Office of Property Assessment sets values.
  • Procurement Department — The City agency that buys goods, services and public-works construction for Philadelphia government through formal bidding, and the rules vendors must follow to win City contracts.
  • Office of Human Resources — The City office that runs civil service hiring, testing, job classification and workforce policy for Philadelphia's roughly 31,000 active municipal employees, under the Civil Service Commission.
  • Law Department — The City of Philadelphia's in-house law firm, led by the City Solicitor, which represents the City in court, drafts contracts and legislation, and collects unpaid taxes and fines.
  • Office of Innovation and Technology — Philadelphia's central technology agency, responsible for City IT infrastructure, cybersecurity, 311 technology, GIS, digital equity programs and AI governance.
  • Chief Administrative Officer — The Mayor's senior official for internal government operations, overseeing HR-related, technology, procurement, records and service-design functions across Philadelphia city government.
  • Department of Records — The City agency that records deeds and other land documents as Philadelphia's Recorder of Deeds, runs the City Archives and publishes official records and regulations.
  • Department of Public Property — The City department that operates and maintains municipal buildings, manages City leases and real estate planning, and deploys security equipment for large public events.
  • Fleet Services — The Department of Fleet Services buys, fuels, repairs and disposes of Philadelphia's roughly 7,000 City vehicles and equipment, from police cars to trash compactors.
  • City Treasurer — The City Treasurer's Office safeguards Philadelphia's cash, issues and manages its debt, runs the Sinking Fund Commission and works to maintain the City's credit ratings.
  • Board of Pensions and Retirement — The board that runs Philadelphia's municipal pension system and invests its roughly $10 billion fund, which stood at 67.7 percent funded in the latest preliminary valuation.
  • Philadelphia City Budget Process — How Philadelphia adopts its annual operating and capital budgets and five-year plan, with the FY2027 budget (a General Fund of about $7 billion) as the worked example.
  • Philadelphia Taxes — A guide to the taxes Philadelphia levies, current rates for FY2027, how much each raises, the multi-year wage and business tax cuts, and relief programs for homeowners.
  • Pennsylvania Intergovernmental Cooperation Authority (PICA) — The state-created authority formed in 1991 to help Philadelphia out of a fiscal crisis, which reviews and approves the City's Five Year Plan each year.
  • Philly311 — Philadelphia's contact center for non-emergency City services and information, reachable by phone, web and mobile app, with interpreters in over 200 languages.
  • Open Data and Transparency Tools — A guide to the public data portals, property search, contract listings, financial disclosures and budget documents residents can use to examine how Philadelphia government works.
  • Office of Economic Opportunity — The Commerce Department office that certifies minority-, woman- and disabled-owned businesses and monitors their participation in Philadelphia city contracts.
  • Commerce Department — Philadelphia's economic development department, which supports small businesses, commercial corridors and large projects and houses the Office of Economic Opportunity.
