Philadelphia Building and Construction Trades Council

From the Philadelphia Civic Wiki, the free guide to how the city works

The Philadelphia Building and Construction Trades Council (often "the Building Trades") is the coordinating body for the city's construction unions, including electricians, plumbers, steamfitters, laborers, carpenters, ironworkers and others. It speaks for the trades in dealings with developers, the city and the state, and is among the most influential labor organizations in Philadelphia politics.[1]

1 Leadership

For years the council's business manager was John Dougherty, who also led IBEW Local 98; the Justice Department's 2024 sentencing release describes a scheme to help elect Dougherty business manager of the Building Trades. After his federal conviction on November 15, 2021, he resigned his Local 98 post, and Ryan Boyer became the council's head, reported in November 2021 by WHYY and the Inquirer as the first Black leader in the council's history.[2] The Philadelphia Council AFL-CIO's executive board lists Boyer for the Building and Construction Trades Council, together with representatives of many construction unions, among them George Pegram (Plumbers Local 690), Jim Snell (Steamfitters Local 420), Mark Lynch Jr. (IBEW Local 98), Esteban Vera Jr. (Laborers Local 57), Sam Staten Jr. (Laborers Local 332), Jim Reilley (Operating Engineers Local 542), Bryan Bush (Sheet Metal Workers Local 19), Rob Cellucci (Insulators Local 14), and Paul Miraglia and Tim Crowther (IUPAT District Council 21).[3]

2 Role in development and public works

The trades advocate for union labor on public projects and large private developments. Tools include project labor agreements, in which the owner and the unions agree on terms for a specific project, and the prevailing wage rules described in Prevailing Wage and Project Labor Agreements. On city public-works contracts, contractors must meet the Davis-Bacon Act and Chapter 17-107 of the Philadelphia Code, submit weekly payroll information, and accept job-site visits by wage compliance officers from the Department of Labor's Office of Labor Standards. Union labor commitments are also a frequent subject of negotiation with developers; see Developers and the Real Estate Lobby, Tax Abatement and Development Incentives and Philadelphia Industrial Development Corporation. The General Building Contractors Association is the employer-side counterpart.[1][4]

3 Training, apprenticeship and diversity

Construction unions run joint apprenticeship programs with their employers. For example, IBEW Local 98 operates an Apprentice Training Academy, and the Philadelphia Council AFL-CIO and the Philadelphia Federation of Teachers have held "Union Experience Fairs" since 2023 to introduce students to union apprenticeship programs. Related city workforce programs are covered in Office of Workforce Development and Office of Economic Opportunity.

4 Politics

The Building Trades Council endorses candidates in city and state races and mobilizes members; Boyer, identified in press coverage as the head of the council, has been a visible participant in local political debates over development proposals. The council's alliances with Philadelphia City Council members and the mayor are discussed in Labor and Politics in Philadelphia.
