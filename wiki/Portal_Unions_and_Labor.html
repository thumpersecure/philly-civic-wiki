Unions and Labor

From the Philadelphia Civic Wiki, the free guide to how the city works

Public and private sector unions, bargaining law, and labor's role in Philadelphia politics.

Articles (19)

  • Organized Labor in Philadelphia — An overview of Philadelphia's labor movement: public-sector, building-trades, transit, health care and service unions, their councils, and how they interact with city government.
  • Public Sector Bargaining in Pennsylvania — How Act 111 (police and fire) and Act 195 (other public employees) govern collective bargaining, strikes and interest arbitration for Philadelphia's city workers.
  • AFSCME District Council 33 — The union of roughly 9,000 blue-collar and related City of Philadelphia employees, led by Greg Boulware, which struck for about eight days in July 2025.
  • AFSCME District Council 47 — The AFSCME council for Philadelphia's professional, technical, administrative and supervisory city workers and employees of nonprofits, universities and courts, led by April Gigetts.
  • Fraternal Order of Police Lodge 5 — The Philadelphia police and sheriff's deputies union, led by Roosevelt Poplar, which bargains with the city under Act 111 binding arbitration.
  • IAFF Local 22 — The union of Philadelphia firefighters, paramedics and EMS personnel, which bargains under Act 111 and was reported in July 2026 to have received a two-year arbitration award.
  • Philadelphia Building and Construction Trades Council — The umbrella body of Philadelphia's construction trade unions, led by business manager Ryan Boyer, and its role in development, public works and politics.
  • IBEW Local 98 — Philadelphia's electricians' local, with James Foy as president and Mark Lynch Jr. as business manager, and the federal convictions of former leaders John Dougherty and Brian Burrows.
  • Philadelphia Federation of Teachers — The union of more than 14,000 Philadelphia public school teachers and staff, led by Arthur Steinberg, and its 2025-2028 contract with the School District.
  • TWU Local 234 and SEPTA Labor — The Transport Workers Union Local 234, the union of SEPTA's transit operating and maintenance employees, its reported December 2025 contract and the history of transit labor disputes.
  • Teamsters in Philadelphia — The International Brotherhood of Teamsters' Philadelphia-area locals, including Locals 107, 623, 830 and 502, covering warehouse, delivery, transit and school-administrator workers.
  • Service and Health Care Unions — Unions of hospital, nursing home, building service, hotel, retail and communications workers in Philadelphia, including 1199C, SEIU Healthcare PA, 32BJ, UNITE HERE Local 274 and CWA.
  • Philadelphia AFL-CIO Council — The Philadelphia Council AFL-CIO, the central labor body of more than 100 local unions representing over 150,000 workers, led by President Daniel Bauder.
  • 2025 DC 33 Strike — The eight-day strike by Philadelphia's blue-collar city workers in July 2025, which suspended trash collection and ended with a three-year contract.
  • Labor and Politics in Philadelphia — How Philadelphia's unions shape elections, budgets and policy: endorsements, political money, building trades influence, police and firefighter unions, and the Dougherty-era prosecutions.
  • Prevailing Wage and Project Labor Agreements — How prevailing wage rules and project labor agreements apply to public construction in Philadelphia, who enforces them, and the policy debate over their costs and benefits.
  • The FOP Contract and Police Discipline — How the police union contract and Act 111 arbitration shape discipline of Philadelphia officers, including reinstatement rates, the 2025 award and oversight disputes.
  • Office of Worker Protections — The City of Philadelphia Department of Labor office that enforces paid sick leave, fair workweek, wage theft and domestic worker laws, strengthened by the 2025 POWER Act.
  • Worker Centers and Labor Advocacy — Community-based worker centers, immigrant organizations and advocacy coalitions in Philadelphia that help workers outside union contracts, including Juntos and domestic worker groups.
