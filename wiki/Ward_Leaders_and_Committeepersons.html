Ward Leaders and Committeepersons

From the Philadelphia Civic Wiki, the free guide to how the city works
Ward Leaders and Committeepersons
TypeElected party offices (unpaid)
Units66 wards; roughly 1,700 voting divisions
SeatsTwo committeepeople per division (3,406 Democratic ward committee seats cited in 2026)
TermFour years; elected in the primary of midterm years (2022, 2026)
Selection of ward leaderElected by the ward's committee members after the primary
Governing lawPennsylvania Election Code; party rules

Ward leaders and committeepersons form the base of each party's organization in Philadelphia. A committeeperson is an elected representative of one voting division within a ward; the committeepeople of a ward together form its committee and elect its ward leader. Despite their low profile, these offices matter because the ward leaders choose the citywide party chair and heavily influence endorsements and Election Day operations.[1]

1 Wards and divisions

Philadelphia is divided into 66 numbered wards, each subdivided into divisions (precincts), which are the places where residents vote. Ward boundaries are an election geography distinct from the ten Council districts; see Council Districts and Wards.

2 Committeepersons

Each party elects a "committeeperson" and a second member, usually one man and one woman, in every division at the primary election held every four years. The Philadelphia Citizen counted 3,406 ward committee seats in the Democratic system in 2026, which equals two seats in each of roughly 1,703 divisions, and reported that at least 1,018 newly elected members would join, about 30 percent of seats.[2] Many seats go unfilled or are won by write-in votes; the City Commissioners publish unofficial write-in results after each primary.[3] Committeepeople canvass voters, distribute party literature, recruit poll workers and vote in the ward's internal elections.

3 Ward leaders

After the primary, the newly elected committee members of each ward meet to elect a ward leader, normally in June. Ward leaders then form the citywide party committee, which elects the chair; see Democratic City Committee and Republican City Committee. Some ward leaders are also elected officials, including members of City Council or the state legislature, which has led reformers to criticize overlap between party and public office. Ward leaders typically recommend candidates for local judgeships and may distribute sample ballots.

4 Election Day roles

Separate from party committee seats, each division elects a Judge of Elections and a Majority and Minority Inspector of Elections in municipal-year elections. The City Commissioners oversee and train these poll workers. Party committeepeople often recruit candidates for these jobs, and vacancies can be filled by court appointment. The distinction matters: committeepeople are party officers; election board members run the polls.

5 How to run

To run for committeeperson, a registered voter living in the division files a short nominating petition with the City Commissioners during the petition window for the primary, or may win by write-in vote. The office is unpaid. Reform organizations such as Reclaim Philadelphia recruit slates, and the 2026 cycle saw a wave of contested races; see Progressive and Grassroots Politics. For general candidate rules, see Running for Office in Philadelphia.
