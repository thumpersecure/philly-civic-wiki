City departments, offices and commissions (52)

BodyWhat it does
Board of Pensions and RetirementThe board that runs Philadelphia's municipal pension system and invests its roughly $10 billion fund, which stood at 67.7 percent funded in the latest preliminary valuation.
Board of Revision of TaxesThe independent board that hears appeals of Philadelphia property assessments and certain tax-exemption and abatement disputes after the Office of Property Assessment sets values.
Chief Administrative OfficerThe Mayor's senior official for internal government operations, overseeing HR-related, technology, procurement, records and service-design functions across Philadelphia city government.
City TreasurerThe City Treasurer's Office safeguards Philadelphia's cash, issues and manages its debt, runs the Sinking Fund Commission and works to maintain the City's credit ratings.
Civil Service CommissionThe Charter-created panel that hears city employee appeals, approves civil service regulations and pay plans, and rules on exemptions and residency waivers.
Commerce DepartmentPhiladelphia's economic development department, which supports small businesses, commercial corridors and large projects and houses the Office of Economic Opportunity.
Commission on Human RelationsPhiladelphia's civil rights enforcement agency, which handles discrimination complaints under the Fair Practices Ordinance and Fair Chance Hiring law.
Department of Behavioral Health and Intellectual disAbility ServicesDBHIDS, the city department that oversees mental health, addiction and intellectual disability services in Philadelphia and funds Medicaid behavioral health through Community Behavioral Health.
Department of Human ServicesPhiladelphia's child welfare and juvenile justice services agency, led by Commissioner Kimberly Ali, working through contracted Community Umbrella Agencies.
Department of Licenses and InspectionsPhiladelphia's code-enforcement agency: building, zoning and trade permits, inspections, licenses, violations and the appeal boards that review them.
Department of Planning and DevelopmentThe Philadelphia city cluster that houses the Planning Commission, Zoning Board of Adjustment, Historical Commission and housing agencies, and leads the mayor's housing plan.
Department of Public HealthPhiladelphia's city-county health agency: disease control, health centers, environmental health, air quality and the Medical Examiner, led by Health Commissioner Dr. Palak Raval-Nelson.
Department of Public PropertyThe City department that operates and maintains municipal buildings, manages City leases and real estate planning, and deploys security equipment for large public events.
Department of RecordsThe City agency that records deeds and other land documents as Philadelphia's Recorder of Deeds, runs the City Archives and publishes official records and regulations.
Division of Housing and Community DevelopmentThe City of Philadelphia's housing funding agency within Planning and Development, which distributes federal and local money for repair, affordable housing and neighborhood programs.
Fair Housing CommissionThe five-member city commission that enforces Philadelphia's Fair Housing Ordinance, holds hearings on rental complaints and verifies landlord licensing and suitability certificates.
Finance DepartmentThe Office of the Director of Finance, the City of Philadelphia's chief financial office, which runs the budget, accounting, payroll, risk management and oversees Revenue, the Treasurer, OPA and Pensions.
Fleet ServicesThe Department of Fleet Services buys, fuels, repairs and disposes of Philadelphia's roughly 7,000 City vehicles and equipment, from police cars to trash compactors.
Free Library of PhiladelphiaPhiladelphia's public library system of 54 libraries, funded mainly by the city and led by President and Director Kelly Richards.
Law DepartmentThe City of Philadelphia's in-house law firm, led by the City Solicitor, which represents the City in court, drafts contracts and legislation, and collects unpaid taxes and fines.
Managing Director's OfficeThe Charter-created office that supervises most of Philadelphia's service departments, from streets and fire to health, prisons and libraries.
Mayor's Cabinet and OfficesHow the mayor's cabinet, senior staff and mayoral offices are organized, which posts the Charter creates, and where to find the current roster.
Medical Examiner's OfficeThe city office that investigates sudden, violent and suspicious deaths in Philadelphia: legal duties, leadership, caseload, and the public record of its leadership turnover and challenges.
Office for People with DisabilitiesThe Charter-created city office, housed in the Chief Administrative Officer's portfolio, that houses ADA compliance and the Mayor's Commission on People with Disabilities.
Office of Arts, Culture and the Creative EconomyThe former name of Creative Philadelphia, the city department for public art, cultural funding and the city's first Cultural Plan, led by Chief Cultural Officer Valerie Gay.
Office of Children and FamiliesThe Parker-era city office that aligns policy and services for children and families, overseeing the Department of Human Services, out-of-school time, PHLpreK and Career Connected Learning.
Office of Community Empowerment and OpportunityPhiladelphia's designated Community Action Agency since 1964, running BenePhilly, Financial Empowerment Centers and anti-poverty programs; led by Executive Director Orlando Rendon.
Office of Economic OpportunityThe Commerce Department office that certifies minority-, woman- and disabled-owned businesses and monitors their participation in Philadelphia city contracts.
Office of Emergency ManagementThe city office that plans for and coordinates responses to disasters, severe weather and large events, and how residents can sign up for alerts and prepare.
Office of Homeless ServicesThe city office that runs Philadelphia's homeless crisis response, shelters, prevention and HUD Continuum of Care, led by Executive Director Cheryl Hill.
Office of Human ResourcesThe City office that runs civil service hiring, testing, job classification and workforce policy for Philadelphia's roughly 31,000 active municipal employees, under the Civil Service Commission.
Office of Immigrant AffairsThe mayoral office that promotes immigrant integration, language access and two immigrant commissions in Philadelphia; led by Director Charlie Elison.
Office of Innovation and TechnologyPhiladelphia's central technology agency, responsible for City IT infrastructure, cybersecurity, 311 technology, GIS, digital equity programs and AI governance.
Office of LGBT AffairsThe city office, now styled the Office of LGBTQ+ Affairs, created by executive order in 2008 and written into the Charter in 2015; led by Executive Director Tyrell Brown.
Office of Property AssessmentThe City agency that determines the market value of every property in Philadelphia, the base for real estate taxes that fund schools and city services; includes how to appeal a value.
Office of SustainabilityThe Philadelphia city office that leads climate action, carbon-neutrality goals, energy efficiency and resilience planning, including the Climate Action Playbook.
Office of Transportation, Infrastructure and Sustainability (OTIS)The Philadelphia mayoral office that coordinates streets, transit, water and climate policy; it traces to the Kenney-era Office of Transportation and Infrastructure Systems.
Office of Violence PreventionThe city office, now part of the Division of Safe Neighborhoods under the Office of Public Safety, that funds and coordinates community-based anti-violence programs.
Office of Worker ProtectionsThe City of Philadelphia Department of Labor office that enforces paid sick leave, fair workweek, wage theft and domestic worker laws, strengthened by the 2025 POWER Act.
Office of Workforce DevelopmentThe city office created in 2018 to coordinate workforce programs, later folded into the Commerce Department's Office of Business Development and Workforce Solutions; works with PhiladelphiaWorks.
Office of the Inspector GeneralPhiladelphia's Office of the Inspector General investigates fraud, waste and misconduct in mayoral agencies and city contracting; learn its history, powers and how to report.
Parks and RecreationPhiladelphia's department managing city parks, recreation centers, playgrounds, pools, trails and the urban forest, including Fairmount Park.
Philadelphia City Planning CommissionThe charter-created commission that writes the city's comprehensive plan and capital program, runs Civic Design Review and registers community organizations.
Philadelphia Department of PrisonsThe city agency that runs Philadelphia's jails: facilities, leadership, population trends, oversight, and the public record on deaths, escapes and staffing.
Philadelphia Fire DepartmentThe city's fire and emergency medical service: structure, leadership, staffing, how residents use it, and its history, including the 2022 Fairmount fire.
Philadelphia International AirportPHL, the city-owned airport run by the Division of Aviation, a major American Airlines hub that has served more than 30 million passengers a year.
Philadelphia Police DepartmentThe city's municipal police force: how it is organized and led, how it is funded, how residents interact with it, and its record of reform and controversy.
Philadelphia Water DepartmentThe city utility that supplies drinking water, treats wastewater and manages stormwater, with rates set by an independent Water Rate Board and assistance through TAP.
Philly311Philadelphia's contact center for non-emergency City services and information, reachable by phone, web and mobile app, with interpreters in over 200 languages.
Procurement DepartmentThe City agency that buys goods, services and public-works construction for Philadelphia government through formal bidding, and the rules vendors must follow to win City contracts.
Revenue DepartmentThe City agency that bills and collects Philadelphia's taxes, water bills and other charges, runs the Philadelphia Tax Center and administers tax and water assistance programs.
Streets DepartmentPhiladelphia's department for trash and recycling collection, street paving, street cleaning, snow removal, traffic signals and the public right of way.
