Tenant and Housing Advocacy Groups

From the Philadelphia Civic Wiki, the free guide to how the city works
Tenant and Housing Advocacy Groups
TypeTenant unions, nonprofits, coalitions and legal-aid-linked networks
ExamplesPhiladelphia Tenants Union (founded 2015); Tenant Union Representative Network (TURN); building-based tenant unions; PACDC; legal-aid and public-interest law organizations
GrowthFrom about five known tenant associations before 2020 to more than 20, per WHYY
Recent policy winSafe and Healthy Homes Act bills passed by City Council in April 2026

Tenant and housing advocacy groups in Philadelphia organize renters, provide education and referral, and lobby on housing law. Roughly half of city residents are renters, many with modest incomes, which makes tenant organizing a significant part of local politics. These groups are private organizations without governmental power. [1]

1 Types of organizations

The field includes (a) tenant unions, which are membership groups of renters in a building, landlord portfolio or neighborhood; (b) citywide organizations such as the Philadelphia Tenants Union, established in 2015, and the Tenant Union Representative Network; (c) legal-aid and public-interest law offices (see Legal Aid and Public Interest Law); (d) housing-counseling agencies and community development corporations; and (e) coalitions that bring these together for legislative campaigns. Historic groups include the North Philadelphia Tenants Union and the Northwest Tenants Organization of earlier decades, which Council has honored by resolution. [2]

2 Growth of tenant unions

WHYY reported that the number of tenant unions in Philadelphia roughly quadrupled since the pandemic, from about five known associations to more than 20, and that they pressed for habitability standards, limits on retaliation and, in one West Philadelphia building, a first collectively bargained lease. Some formed autonomously in a single building; others emerged from partnerships with tenant-rights organizations. [1]

3 Policy campaigns

Advocates supported the right to counsel in eviction cases and the City's eviction-diversion program (see Eviction Diversion and Landlord-Tenant Court). A recent campaign centered on the Safe and Healthy Homes Act, bills by Councilmember Nicolas O'Rourke. The legislation provides rent abatement when a landlord lacks an active rental license or fails to repair code violations in a timely way, protects tenants against retaliation for complaints, requires "good cause" for lease non-renewal and increases penalties for repeated violations. A lawsuit by two landlords in March 2026 alleging violations of the state Sunshine Act delayed a Council vote; after a settlement the bills returned to committee, and Council passed the final bills in April 2026. See Rental Licensing and Landlord-Tenant Law in Philadelphia. [3]

4 Allies, opponents and criticism

Tenant groups frequently work with progressive Council members and organizations (see Progressive and Grassroots Politics) and labor unions. Their principal opponents on housing law are landlord and property-owner associations (see Landlord and Property Owner Associations) and parts of the real estate sector. Opponents argue that added regulation raises costs and may reduce rental supply; advocates argue that it protects safety and stability. Both sides appeal to data from Licenses and Inspections and the courts.

5 Getting help or joining

Tenants can call the Philly Tenant Hotline, reported as (267) 443-2500, or the Fair Housing Commission for rent-related disputes (see Fair Housing Commission). Organizing basics appear in How to Organize a Block or Neighborhood Group. This is general information, not legal advice.

See also: Philadelphia Housing Authority, Office of Homeless Services, Division of Housing and Community Development.
