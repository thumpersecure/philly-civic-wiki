Land Use, Streets and Utilities

From the Philadelphia Civic Wiki, the free guide to how the city works

Permits, zoning, inspections, streets, water, parks, libraries, airport and gas.

Articles (15)

  • Department of Licenses and Inspections — Philadelphia's code-enforcement agency: building, zoning and trade permits, inspections, licenses, violations and the appeal boards that review them.
  • Department of Planning and Development — The Philadelphia city cluster that houses the Planning Commission, Zoning Board of Adjustment, Historical Commission and housing agencies, and leads the mayor's housing plan.
  • Philadelphia City Planning Commission — The charter-created commission that writes the city's comprehensive plan and capital program, runs Civic Design Review and registers community organizations.
  • Zoning Board of Adjustment — The seven-member Philadelphia board that hears appeals of zoning permit refusals, grants variances and special exceptions, and is itself appealable to the Court of Common Pleas.
  • Philadelphia Zoning Code — Title 14 of the Philadelphia Code, the 2012 rewrite of the city's zoning rules, its districts, overlays, approval paths and community-notice requirements.
  • Philadelphia Historical Commission — The city body that designates historic buildings and districts, maintains the Philadelphia Register of Historic Places and reviews permits for work on registered properties.
  • Registered Community Organizations — Neighborhood groups registered with the Planning Commission that must be notified of, and can comment on, Philadelphia zoning and design-review cases.
  • Streets Department — Philadelphia's department for trash and recycling collection, street paving, street cleaning, snow removal, traffic signals and the public right of way.
  • Philadelphia Water Department — The city utility that supplies drinking water, treats wastewater and manages stormwater, with rates set by an independent Water Rate Board and assistance through TAP.
  • Parks and Recreation — Philadelphia's department managing city parks, recreation centers, playgrounds, pools, trails and the urban forest, including Fairmount Park.
  • Free Library of Philadelphia — Philadelphia's public library system of 54 libraries, funded mainly by the city and led by President and Director Kelly Richards.
  • Office of Sustainability — The Philadelphia city office that leads climate action, carbon-neutrality goals, energy efficiency and resilience planning, including the Climate Action Playbook.
  • Office of Transportation, Infrastructure and Sustainability (OTIS) — The Philadelphia mayoral office that coordinates streets, transit, water and climate policy; it traces to the Kenney-era Office of Transportation and Infrastructure Systems.
  • Philadelphia International Airport — PHL, the city-owned airport run by the Division of Aviation, a major American Airlines hub that has served more than 30 million passengers a year.
  • Philadelphia Gas Works — The nation's largest municipally owned natural gas utility, now regulated by the Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission rather than the Philadelphia Gas Commission.
