Law Department

The Law Department is the legal counsel for the whole City government. Led by the City Solicitor, it represents the City and its officials and employees in litigation, negotiates and drafts contracts, prepares legislation, advises on regulatory compliance, represents the City in child-welfare and health matters, and collects unpaid taxes, fines and other debts.[1] It is both a municipal and a county law office, because Philadelphia is a consolidated city-county (see City-County Consolidation). The Solicitor is appointed by the Mayor and, as the 2026 transition shows, is subject to confirmation by City Council.

1 Leadership and recent transition

Mayor Cherelle Parker named Renee Garcia, a litigation chair at a private firm, as City Solicitor in December 2023, and she served from the start of the administration.[2] The Philadelphia Inquirer reported her resignation on July 28, 2026.[3] On September 3, 2026 the City announced that Monique Myatt Galloway had begun a transition into the role of Acting City Solicitor on September 2, pending City Council confirmation. Galloway had been an Assistant U.S. Attorney in the Civil Division of the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Eastern District of Pennsylvania since November 2023 and earlier served as regional counsel for the General Services Administration and Federal Transit Administration and as a Navy trial attorney; she also served on the Pennsylvania State Ethics Commission.[4] See US Attorney's Office, Eastern District of Pennsylvania.

2 Structure and practice areas

The department's website organizes its work into four main groups: Compliance and Legislation, Corporate and Tax, Litigation, and Social Services.[1] A 2025 year-in-review booklet describes units including Tax Litigation and Collections, Property Assessment, Boards and Commissions, Commercial Law, Real Estate and Development, Compliance, HIPAA and Privacy, Legislation and Legal Counsel, Right-to-Know, and E-Discovery and Data Strategies, and reports more than 300 attorneys, over 120 professional staff and more than 50 interns.[5] Council appropriated about $38.5 million from the General Fund for FY27, with about $23.7 million for personnel.[6] The Law Department also bills other funds, such as Water, for work done on their behalf.

3 Role in city government

The Law Department is involved in nearly every City function. It reviews contracts for form and legality (see Procurement Department and Understanding City Contracts and Bids), drafts bills for Council members, and issues legal opinions to officials. It defends the City in civil-rights, personal-injury and employment suits, with settlements and judgments paid through the Finance Department's indemnities appropriation. In tax matters, its Tax Litigation and Collections work supports the Revenue Department, including filing cases and pursuing Sheriff's sales. In land-use matters it represents the City before boards and commissions and in the Commonwealth Court. Its Right-to-Know unit advises agencies on requests under state law (see Right-to-Know Law and Public Records). During the federal policy changes of 2025 and 2026, the Budget Office reported working closely with Law to respond to changes in federal funding.[7]

4 Conflicts and limits

The Solicitor's client is the City as an institution, not individual officials or members of the public, and the Department does not give legal advice to residents. Independent elected officials such as the City Controller and the District Attorney are not Law Department clients in the same sense, and disputes among City officials sometimes lead to outside counsel or court rulings. Ethical standards for City lawyers are set by the Pennsylvania Rules of Professional Conduct, and the Board of Ethics enforces the City's ethics code. Tenants and residents seeking representation should look to Legal Aid and Public Interest Law.

5 Dealing with the Law Department

Claims against the City, such as injuries on public property, are subject to notice requirements and statutes under Pennsylvania law and should be pursued with a lawyer. Requests for City records go first to the agency that holds them; see How to Request Public Records. Organizational contacts are available at the department's offices at 1515 Arch Street.
